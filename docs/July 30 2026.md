Key Discussion Topics
1. AI Benchmark Development for Clinical Trial Workflows
The group emphasized that benchmark development is becoming a foundational component for evaluating AI systems in pharmaceutical statistics and clinical development.
Key points
AI performance is ultimately driven by the quality of the benchmark and evaluation criteria provided.
Benchmark design should capture a broad range of realistic protocol and statistical scenarios rather than narrowly optimized tasks.
There is a need to continuously evolve benchmarks to prevent models from overfitting to specific test cases.
Benchmark development should be viewed as an iterative process, with increasing complexity and coverage over time.
Future benchmarks may themselves be partially generated or refined with AI assistance.
Strategic importance
Participants agreed that:
Benchmarks provide lasting value independent of rapid changes in underlying models.
Evaluation frameworks and domain-specific assessment criteria are likely to remain important even as foundation models improve.
2. Domain Knowledge as a Competitive Advantage
The group discussed limitations of current frontier models in pharmaceutical development.
Observations
Publicly available protocols and papers provide useful training material, but do not capture the full pharmaceutical development context.
AI systems often lack knowledge of:
Cross-functional decision making
Clinical development strategy
Protocol design rationale
Statistical planning workflows
Regulatory and operational considerations
Conclusion
Success will require translating pharmaceutical expertise into:
Structured benchmarks
Evaluation rubrics
Domain ontologies
AI-consumable knowledge representations
Participants viewed these assets as more durable and differentiated than building narrow applications that may be rapidly displaced by future model capabilities.
3. Ontology and Structured Knowledge Representation
A major topic was the role of ontologies in enabling enterprise-grade AI systems.
Discussion highlights
Pharmaceutical workflows contain complex relationships among entities, rules, assumptions, analyses, and decisions.
AI performs better when information is provided in structured, semantic formats rather than raw documents.
Ontologies can connect previously isolated datasets and specifications.
Concepts were compared to object-oriented programming where entities, attributes, and relationships are explicitly defined.
Potential applications
ADaM specifications
Dataset metadata
Analysis traceability
Protocol-to-analysis linkage
AI-native documentation and workflow automation
Future direction
The group expressed interest in a future session dedicated specifically to ontology implementation and practical use cases.
4. Relationship Between Ontology and CDISC/Standards Evolution
Participants noted potential alignment with emerging standards efforts.
Themes discussed
Increasing standardization creates opportunities for automation.
Ontology-based representations could serve as AI-native layers on top of existing standards.
Future workflows may shift from:
human-authored specifications → AI interpretation
toward
structured knowledge models → automated generation of human-readable specifications.
5. Agentic AI and Continuous Learning Concepts
The discussion included examples of systems that automatically learn from prior tasks.
Ideas explored
AI-generated retrospectives and lessons learned.
Automated skill extraction from repeated workflows.
Continuous refinement of knowledge representations.
Risks of over-engineering or generating excessive low-value abstractions.
Participants agreed that maintaining and curating generated knowledge remains important.
6. Experiences Using AI for Statistical Research
Several participants shared practical experiences using AI agents.
Common use cases
Simulation studies
Stress testing
Applied statistical research
Rapid code generation
Result interpretation
Observations
AI substantially accelerates routine simulation work.
Current systems are significantly more capable than those available a few years ago.
Human review remains necessary, particularly for validation and contextual understanding.
7. Open-Source Models and Enterprise Adoption
The group discussed trends in open-source AI.
Key observations
Open-source models are becoming increasingly capable while operating at much lower cost.
Local deployment is becoming practical for many research and enterprise applications.
Model costs continue to decline rapidly.
Future differentiation may come less from model access and more from proprietary knowledge assets and structured domain expertise.
Consensus
Long-term value is expected to come from:
Domain ontologies
Structured pharmaceutical knowledge
Evaluation frameworks
Benchmark datasets
rather than from access to any specific model.
